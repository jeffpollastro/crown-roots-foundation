About Crown Roots Foundation

Expanding the worldview of Pocono Mountain youth — because the mountain is not the whole world, and every kid deserves to know that.

Our Mission

Crown Roots Foundation exists to expand possibility for Pocono Mountain youth. Basketball is our gateway — mentorship, academics, and worldview expansion are the real work. Where you grow up shouldn’t determine whether you grow, and every young person on the mountain deserves the development, mentorship, and college-readiness pathways that show them how big the world really is. The Work Is the Reward.

Our Vision

A Pocono Mountain where kids know their world isn't confined to the zip code they were born in. Where academics and character are championed with the same energy as athletics. Where girls don't have to transfer to The Valley or other states' schools to compete, families don't choose between a part-time job and off-season training, and mentors are telling kids every day: you can be great in the classroom, on the court, and in life.

How We Work

Two connected efforts carry the mission: United gets kids in the gym and the classroom. Crown Hub tells their story to the world.

Beyond the Data: What We See on the Ground

The numbers tell part of the story. The rest plays out in gyms, living rooms, and transfer paperwork across the Poconos every year.

The Off-Season Gap

Coaches are expected to develop girls within the regular season alone. Off-season open gyms sit nearly empty — the year-round culture Valley programs take for granted doesn’t exist here.

Work Over Basketball

In economically disadvantaged households, a part-time job isn’t optional. No local pathway has made basketball worth the tradeoff.

Talent Leaves

Standout players transfer to Valley or out-of-state programs with the infrastructure the mountain lacks. We develop talent, then watch it walk out the door.

No One's Watching

Without local role models or visible pathways, girls on the mountain aren’t growing up as fans or aspiring players. The pipeline was never built.

What the Numbers Mean

“Economically Disadvantaged” refers to students eligible for free or reduced-price lunch under the National School Lunch Program. A family of four qualifies at a household income of roughly $55,500 or less (185% of the federal poverty line). When a school shows 49% economically disadvantaged, it means nearly half of its students come from families where paying for groceries is a daily calculation — let alone $1,500–$5,000 per season for AAU basketball.

On the mountain, that 49% average is spread across every school. There is no wealthy neighboring district to absorb the cost or subsidize programs. In the Valley, high-poverty districts like Allentown (91%) sit next to affluent ones like Parkland (23%) — and all of them are within 20 minutes of multiple AAU programs, YMCAs, and training facilities.

Pocono Mountain School District has committed nearly $4.9 million to gym and athletic facility upgrades in recent years — including a $1.49M gym renovation at East High School and a $3.38M Wellness Center at West. These investments show the district values athletics. But a renovated gym without year-round programming is just a building. Without structured off-season development, the mountain’s girls still have nowhere to grow — no matter how new the floor is.
