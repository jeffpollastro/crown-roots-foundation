About Crown Roots Foundation

Expanding the worldview of Pocono Mountain youth, because the mountain is not the whole world, and every kid deserves to know that.

Our Mission

Crown Roots Foundation exists to expand possibility for Pocono Mountain youth. We lead with academics: helping students build a strong record, walking families through college applications and financial aid, and taking students to see campuses for themselves. Where you grow up shouldn’t determine whether you grow, and every young person on the mountain deserves the information, mentorship, and college pathways that show them how big the world really is. The Work Is the Reward.

Our Vision

A Pocono Mountain where kids know their world isn't confined to the zip code they were born in. Where academics and character are championed with the same energy as athletics. Where every family sees its real college price before ruling a school out, students walk a college campus long before they have to choose one, and mentors are telling kids every day: you can be great in the classroom, in your community, and in life.

How We Work

Two connected efforts carry the mission: United walks students and families through academics, college applications, and campus visits. Crown Hub tells their story to the world.

Why We Go on Campus Visits

A college website can’t tell a student what it feels like to belong somewhere. On a campus visit, students walk the grounds, sit in a classroom, stand in a dorm room, and meet people who were in their shoes a few years ago. College stops being an idea and becomes a place they can picture themselves. For some of our students, it’s the first college campus they’ve ever set foot on.

Why Youngstown State Is High on Our List

Youngstown State is number two on our Crown Picks Top 10, and it’s the school we know best. Three students we’ve worked with attended its engineering program for nearly $0 by stacking scholarships, and one was paid to attend. A Pennsylvania student pays only $360 a year more in tuition than an Ohio student, the YSU Foundation awards more than 1,100 scholarships through one application, and engineering, nursing, business, social work, and art are all here and accredited. YSU is also an NCAA Division I school, and its campus is equipped like one. Every student benefits from that, not just the athletes: game days and school spirit, and recreation and wellness facilities open to all students.

Just as important, it fits our kids. YSU sits in a small city just over the Pennsylvania line, in a region that looks and feels a lot like home. The campus is big enough to offer real programs and small enough that students don’t get lost. For a student from a small Pocono town, a massive university with huge lecture halls, or a campus in a big city, can mean a lot of change at once: getting around, the cost of living, and finding help when you need it. Students who feel at home are more likely to stay, and staying is what turns a campus visit into a degree.

Our Community

Beyond the Data: What We See on the Ground

The numbers tell part of the story. The rest plays out at kitchen tables, in guidance offices, and on college websites across the Poconos every year.

The Sticker Price Stops Families

Families see a sticker price or a national average and rule a college out before they ever see their real number. The grants and scholarships that change the math never come up.

Work Comes First

In economically disadvantaged households, a part-time job isn’t optional. Time for college research, essays, and scholarship applications gets squeezed out.

Nobody Knows Where to Look

Foundation scholarships, local awards, and early deadlines can bring a college close to $0. Three students we’ve worked with attended Youngstown State engineering for nearly nothing. Most families never hear how.

College Feels Far Away

Many students have never set foot on a college campus. It’s hard to picture yourself somewhere you’ve never been, which is why we take them there.

The Opportunity Gap

These numbers don’t tell the story of talent. They tell the story of access. When kids grow up without information, mentors, and programs, the mountain becomes the whole world. It shouldn’t be.

What the Numbers Mean

“Economically Disadvantaged” refers to students eligible for free or reduced-price lunch under the National School Lunch Program. A family of four qualifies at a household income of roughly $55,500 or less (185% of the federal poverty line). When a school shows 49% economically disadvantaged, it means nearly half of its students come from families where paying for groceries is a daily calculation, let alone $1,500–$5,000 per season for AAU basketball.

On the mountain, that 49% average is spread across every school. There is no wealthy neighboring district to absorb the cost or subsidize programs. In the Valley, high-poverty districts like Allentown (91%) sit next to affluent ones like Parkland (23%), and all of them are within 20 minutes of multiple AAU programs, YMCAs, and training facilities.

Pocono Mountain School District has committed nearly $4.9 million to gym and athletic facility upgrades in recent years, including a $1.49M gym renovation at East High School and a $3.38M Wellness Center at West. Without structured off-season development, the mountain’s girls still have nowhere to grow, no matter how new the floor is.
