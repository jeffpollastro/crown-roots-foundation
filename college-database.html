The Problem This Solves

$45,000
What families see: "Average Financial Aid"
$18,000
What families miss: "What you still pay each year"

Every college posts a price, and most post a generous-sounding aid figure. Neither tells a family what it will actually pay. Grants, merit scholarships, and need-based aid are real and within reach for many Pocono students, but families rarely see them added up against the full cost. The problem isn't a lack of opportunity. It's a lack of information.

What's missing is The Gap: the full yearly cost (tuition, fees, housing, food, books, travel, and personal costs) minus grants. It is what a family has to cover through savings, work, or loans. For a Pocono family of five earning $56,000, East Stroudsburg's own calculator shows a Gap of $16,239 a year for a student with a 3.5 GPA. In/Root puts that number first, for every school.

How It Works

1. Enter Your Income

Choose your family income range. Every school's Gap updates to match.

2. Lowest Gap First

Results are sorted by what your family would pay, not by prestige or sticker price.

3. Narrow the List

Set a maximum Gap, a state, or a distance from home. Show only no-loan schools or HBCUs, or search by name. View results as a list or on a map.

4. Compare

Add up to four schools and see their costs side by side.

Hidden Value Schools

Selective, high-endowment schools within about five hours of the Poconos that can cost a family less than East Stroudsburg University. These prices only matter to a student who is admitted, and admission takes top grades and test scores: Princeton, Penn, and Swarthmore admit fewer than one in ten applicants. The ★ Under $12K badge marks schools where The Gap falls below $12,000 a year for the income you select.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which colleges have no-loan financial aid policies?

Many colleges with endowments above $500,000 per student, including several Ivy League and other highly selective universities, have no-loan financial aid policies: they replace student loans with grants. For a Pocono family of five earning $56,000, Princeton University costs $0. Princeton covers tuition, housing, food, fees, and a travel allowance with grants for families earning under $150,000. East Stroudsburg University's own calculator puts the same family at $11,745 a year for a student with a 4.0 GPA and $16,239 for a 3.5 student.

What is "The Gap" in college financial aid?

The Gap is what a family pays each year after grants and scholarships: the full cost of attendance (tuition, fees, housing, food, books, travel, and personal costs) less grants, before loans. Loans are not counted as aid. For a Pocono family of five earning $56,000, East Stroudsburg University's own calculator shows a Gap of $11,745 a year for a 4.0 student and $16,239 for a 3.5 student. Several selective schools with large endowments cost the same family less, some $0. In/Root sorts every school by lowest Gap first.

Are elite colleges cheaper than state schools for low-income families?

For a student who gets in, often yes. For a Pocono family of five earning $56,000, Princeton University costs $0 and Lafayette College costs $4,047 a year, while East Stroudsburg University's own calculator shows $11,745 for a 4.0 student. Schools with large endowments can meet a family's full financial need, so what they cost can be far below their sticker price. The catch is admission: these schools take top grades and test scores.

What is the Crown Top 10?

The Crown Top 10 is our list of ten colleges that a typical Crown Roots student (a 3.2 to 3.7 GPA and about an 1100 SAT) can realistically get into, that a Pocono family earning under $75,000 can pay for, and that lead to careers our students ask about. We rank on the full yearly cost for a sample family after grants, before loans, using each school's own cost calculator, and on what graduates of specific programs earn. Very selective schools that can cost little or nothing are listed separately as Hidden Value Schools.

Where does this database get its data?

All figures come from federal and institutional sources: IPEDS (Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System) for tuition and financial aid; each school's own net price calculator for the prices on our school lists; NACUBO for endowment data; and manual institutional verification of no-loan policies.
